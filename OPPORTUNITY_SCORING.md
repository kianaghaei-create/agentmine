# Opportunity scoring — v1 (decision aid, not probability)

Score each criterion 0–5 with dated evidence. Weighted opportunity score = 20 × sum(weight × rating), where weights sum to 1. A high score is **not** a success probability.

| Dimension | Weight | 0 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Willingness to pay | 25% | No buyer signal | Credible buyer interest | Repeat paid transactions with retention |
| Addressable economics | 20% | Tiny/unreachable | Plausible reachable segment | Large reachable spend, measured |
| Scalability / low-touch | 20% | Bespoke labor | Partly automated | Repeatable self-serve delivery |
| Differentiation | 15% | Commodity | Some edge | Defensible distribution/data advantage |
| Unit economics | 10% | Negative/unknown | Modeled viable | Verified attractive contribution margin |
| Speed to first sale | 10% | Long/unproven | Pilot in weeks | Paid sale already completed |

## Separate evidence score (0–100)
- 0–19: speculation, no external validation.
- 20–39: credible sources and buyer pain, no purchase commitment.
- 40–59: interviews, demonstrated workflow and explicit buying intent; no repeat revenue.
- 60–79: paid pilots / real transactions, economics partially observed.
- 80–100: repeat paid usage, retention, acquisition and costs measured across multiple customers.

Evidence score is an ordinal rubric, not a calibrated probability. Record source links, customer counts, sample bias, dates, costs and confidence.

## Decision matrix
- High opportunity, low evidence: **cheap validation experiment**, not a goldmine.
- High opportunity, high evidence: **priority candidate for investment**.
- Low opportunity, high evidence: **possible niche cashflow business**, consider constraints.
- Low opportunity, low evidence: **backlog / stop**.

## Mandatory gates before labeling 'validated opportunity'
1. At least one external buyer has paid or made a binding paid commitment.
2. Legal right to access and resell underlying data confirmed.
3. Gross margin and support/maintenance costs modeled honestly.
4. Customer acquisition channel identified and tested.
5. Repeatability, retention or a concrete repeat-use reason observed.

## Every candidate report
- Problem, buyer, trigger, current workaround, proposed offer.
- Opportunity score, evidence score and dated source evidence.
- TAM assumptions separate from reachable market.
- Estimated price, gross margin, CAC, payback, hours/week, regulatory risk.
- Most dangerous unverified assumption; cheapest falsification test.
- Continue / pivot / stop and required human approval.

**Never** interpret 90/100 as a 90% chance of success. Avoid invented revenue projections. Compare ranges and downside exposure.
