# Emerging opportunity radar

## Mission
Find market gaps **before** they become obvious by monitoring capability advances, research, open-source adoption, bottlenecks, regulation and unmet commercial needs. This complements, rather than replaces, current x402/API market research.

## Research funnel
1. **Signal:** dated primary research paper, benchmark, GitHub project, standards proposal, regulation or real customer complaint.
2. **Capability shift:** what agents can newly do, with limitations and adoption timeline.
3. **Second-order bottleneck:** what fails or becomes expensive if this capability spreads?
4. **Economic buyer:** who loses money or time, how often, and what alternatives exist?
5. **Opportunity:** product or service that removes bottleneck, distribution path, data rights and pricing hypothesis.
6. **Falsification:** cheapest test, explicit pivot/kill criterion.

## Sources to examine (not automatic proof of demand)
- arXiv, conference proceedings and credible technical evaluations
- GitHub repositories, releases, dependency trends and issues
- MCP and agent interoperability specifications
- Agent security, observability, reliability and evaluation incident reports
- Enterprise adoption studies and buyer discussions
- Procurement, compliance, identity and payment standards

## Initial watch themes
- Verifiable task completion and outcome-based billing
- Agent identity, delegated authority and spend permissions
- Trust and reputation between agent services
- Audit trails, rollback and exception recovery
- Agent-native service discovery and procurement
- Data provenance, freshness guarantees and liability
- Human oversight bottlenecks in regulated workflows

## Rules
- A published paper is a **signal**, not customer validation.
- Forecasts are hypotheses with dated evidence and explicit uncertainty.
- Every opportunity must be scored using OPPORTUNITY_SCORING.md.
- Always compare an unconventional adjacent business with the current DaaS hypothesis.
- Keep a weekly top-5 watchlist and a separate evidence log; do not conflate excitement with revenue.
